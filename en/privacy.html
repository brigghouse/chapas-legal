Privacy

Chapas · last updated: 25 October 2026

The summary, in four lines

Chapas works without an account, and without an account nothing leaves your phone. If you create an account, your email, your album and your photos are kept, and nothing else. No adverts, no analytics, no trackers, and we sell nothing to anyone. Photos are cleaned before upload: the GPS tag your phone adds stays out.

Who is responsible

Chapas is a personal project, not a company. The data controller is the person who publishes the app, and the contact for anything to do with privacy is ianbrighouse4@gmail.com.

No account, no processing

The whole album — sticking caps, the collections, the badges, the rounds — works without signing in. In that mode everything lives in your phone's own storage and is never sent to a server. Uninstall the app and it is gone. That is also why signing in is optional and not a step you are made to take when you open it.

What is kept if you create an account

The account does two things: bring your album back if you change phones, and share rounds. For that, it keeps:

DataWhere it comes fromWhat for
Email address You, or Apple or Google when you sign in To identify the account. If you use Sign in with Apple and choose to hide your email, what reaches us is Apple's relay address, never yours.
Your album: beers, times you noted them, ratings, gifts and rounds What you type in the app Backup and sync across your devices.
Cap photos The camera, if you choose to take a photo So the card carries its photo. They are re‑encoded on the phone before upload, which strips the EXIF metadata, GPS included.
Creation and modification dates of each row The app itself To know what changed since the last sync. Without this there is no sync.

What is not kept, though it could be

  • Your location. The app never asks for location permission and does not have it. And since photos are re‑encoded, it does not slip in through the back door either.
  • Your friends' names. When you give away a double and type a name, that name stays on your phone and is not uploaded. It is someone else's data and they have not given permission.
  • Analytics, profiles or advertising. There is no SDK from anyone measuring what you tap. There is no advertising identifier.
  • Your date of birth. The app is rated for adults and asks whether you are one the first time you open it. The answer and the date you gave it are kept on your phone. Nothing more: that is enough, and it is one personal detail fewer.
  • Passwords. There are none. You sign in with Apple, with Google or with a link sent to your email.

Permissions the app asks for

  • Camera: only while you photograph a cap or read a barcode. No video is recorded and nothing is sent unless you stick the card.
  • Motion sensors: so the cap tilts with you on the card screen. That reading is used on screen and thrown away; it is neither kept nor sent.
  • Vibration: the little tap when you stick a card.

Legal basis

  • Performing what you asked for (art. 6(1)(b) GDPR and UK GDPR): keeping your album and photos is the service you requested when you created the account. Without an account there is no processing to justify.
  • Legal obligation (art. 6(1)(c)): the age declaration exists because the app stores and the rules on alcohol content require it.

Who else touches it

One provider: Supabase, which hosts the database, the user registry and the photos, on servers in the European Union. It acts as a processor and does not use this data for anything of its own. If you sign in with Apple or Google, that company knows you signed in to Chapas, just as when you use its button in any other app; what they return to us is an identifier and an email.

There is nobody else. Data is neither shared with third parties nor sold.

For how long

For as long as you keep the account. When you delete it, the rows and the photos are deleted at that moment, not “within X days”. The provider's backups rotate and expire on their own within 30 days at most.

Your rights, and where the button is

The GDPR and the UK GDPR give you the rights of access, portability, rectification, erasure, restriction and objection. The two you can exercise in one tap are inside the app, in the Account tab:

  • Take my data with me — gives you a JSON file with everything, ready to share or keep (access and portability, arts. 15 and 20).
  • Delete account — deletes account, album and photos, with nobody in between (erasure, art. 17).

If you would rather ask from outside the app, there is the deletion page. To rectify, restrict or object, write to ianbrighouse4@gmail.com; you will hear back within the month the regulation sets. And if you think we have not got it right, you can complain to the Spanish Data Protection Agency (AEPD) or, in the United Kingdom, to the Information Commissioner's Office (ICO).

Minors

Chapas is an app about beer and is rated for adults (18+ on the App Store and the equivalent on Google Play). It is not aimed at minors and they should not be allowed to use it.

Changes

If this changes, the date above changes and the notice appears in the app before the change affects anything you already have stored.
